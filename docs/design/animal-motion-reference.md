# Animal motion references and native authoring

The owner supplied Quaternius' [Ultimate Animated Animal Pack](https://quaternius.com/packs/ultimateanimatedanimals.html)
in `bible/models/mobs/quanternius_animal_pack`. The original archive remains there.
Its CC0 motion references are cataloged with archive and individual FBX SHA-256
fingerprints in [quaternius-animal-reference.json](quaternius-animal-reference.json).
The catalog is generated by `scripts/anim/blender_catalog_animal_pack.py`.

Run the cataloger with the local Blender executable:

```text
blender --background --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/anim/blender_catalog_animal_pack.py
```

It inventories the available animals and their actual imported action durations,
then samples the wolf's Walk, Gallop and Attack landmarks into
`tmp/animal-motion-reference/wolf-landmarks.json`. A reference-only GLB, including
the imported actions, is exported beside that file for visual comparison. Neither
donor geometry nor donor skin weights are installed in the game's animal assets.

## Using the pack for another animal

Start with the catalog to select a species and motion with an appropriate body
plan. Inspect its full cycle, including support, swing, anticipation, contact and
recovery. Compare landmark travel relative to the animal's height, not absolute
FBX units. The reference wolf's walk lasts about 1.07 seconds; its attack uses a
front step, supported hind pair and a large head/chest excursion over about
1.33 seconds. These supply timing and weight-transfer references for the native
wolf revision. There is no bear in this pack; canine poses are not transplanted
onto the bear skeleton.

Map anatomical joints using the actual hierarchy and positions. The Quaternius
wolf has named shoulders, three neck segments and separate IK controls; the native
Smart Rig has anonymous bones and different segment lengths. Left/right naming
also uses different signs on the lateral axis. Copying local rotations by name
or replacing a third leg segment with a two-bone chain is not a valid retarget.

Keep the native mesh, weights and materials. Author native paw targets and body
motion offline, then export ordinary clips through the existing Blender bakers
and character asset pipeline. Compression remains Meshopt plus KTX2. Preserve
the existing manifest dispatcher and use support-paw velocity to set locomotion
reference speeds at the target presentation height.

## Flow revision

`scripts/anim/animal_motion_curves.py` shares timing math between the bear and
wolf authors. Recovery strokes match support velocity and acceleration at paw
contact. Lift and wrist motion fade smoothly into contact. Bear strike/recovery
arcs retain a matched nonzero passage tangent, while the deliberate anticipation
and double-strike loading pause remain. Trunk, head and tail counterbalance with
small phase offsets instead of starting and stopping together.

The wolf adds working scapulae, a wider stride, broader neck travel, a supported
forepaw step for each bite, and longer attack recovery. Gallop paw paths are
centered beneath each limb so the hock avoids both full folding and excessive
extension. Bigger motion must not force the solver onto either reach limit.
No scale channel is used to expand the trunk.

Also validate actual renderer gait selection at the mob's world speed.
`updateLocomotion` uses general humanoid run thresholds; a compact native animal
can need a run gait below that threshold. The goat opts into `VisualDef.gait`
through the pure `model_gait_core` seam. Its hysteresis changes only dry walk/run
presentation, leaving simulation speed, global tracking and other states
unchanged. Check that `referenceSpeed * locomotionTimeScale` matches travel
without a cadence clamp. A preview's forced `running` flag alone does not prove
the animal chooses the same gait while chasing in game.

`tests/animal_motion_flow.test.ts`, both native animal asset suites and
`tests/quadruped_death_pose.test.ts` check the exported behavior, including
follow-through, contact, native joints and retained relaxed death poses.
Before/after clips and validation are in
[the motion-flow evidence](../screenshots/animal-motion-flow/verification.md).

## Horn contact and muzzle reach

The contact revision separately samples Bull and Stag `Attack_Headbutt` from the
owner's cached FBXs. Their neck and head landmarks show an early head bow followed
by torso drive and a later head recovery. The sampled actions last about 1.03 and
0.80 seconds respectively. These are references for sequencing, not rotations
copied onto the native goat. Its ram retains a longer crown-first contact so the
horns remain readable through the forward body drive.

The goat now holds its bow beyond torso contact, with a stronger bow for Ram than
for the lateral headbutts. Crown travel, rather than nose travel, measures its
attack reach. The wolf counter-rotates its upper neck to project the muzzle while
closing the jaw, then withdraws the head after the chest starts returning. Both
use offset quintic releases, keeping velocity and acceleration soft at the ends
without scaling the neck or torso. Only the attack clips are replaced.

`tests/animal_contact_pose.test.ts` checks head-relative reach and crown-first
contact in the final compressed assets. See [contact evidence](../screenshots/animal-contact/verification.md)
for the before/after comparisons, sampled ungulate references and preservation
checks. The native meshes, weights, textures and all other clips are retained.
